# Translator glossary

Heartpact is an app for one couple. Each partner dares the other to exercise ("20 sit-ups by 8:00"); at that time the
partner's phone rings like an alarm and the camera counts the reps. Each dare kept becomes a heart: each partner
sticks on their half, and the hearts collect on a "sticker page". The couple also shares a photo album, a food log
with calorie estimates from photos, a daily question, an optional cycle tracker and pregnancy mode, location
sharing and small games. Heartpact Plus is the paid subscription.

Voice: warm, playful, plain and short. Speak to one person, informally. Buttons are 1-3 words. Never stiffer than
the English.

Placeholders like `{partner}`, `{name}`, `{count}`, `{minutes}` must stay exactly as written (Latin letters, same
braces) but can move anywhere in the sentence. `{count}` in a plural entry is the number.

- French: tutoiement (tu), French spacing before ? ! : is optional; use plain apostrophe ' like the English.
- Portuguese: Brazilian, você.
- Spanish: Latin American neutral, tú (not vos, not vosotros).
- Arabic: Modern Standard Arabic, simple and friendly. Address the reader without gendered forms where possible
  (prefer imperatives/nouns that read naturally for anyone; when unavoidable use the masculine default). Keep
  numbers as placeholders; don't add Arabic-Indic digits.
- Hindi: Devanagari, conversational Hindi as people text (तुम), English loanwords are fine where Indians use them
  (app, photo, sync, calories, camera).
